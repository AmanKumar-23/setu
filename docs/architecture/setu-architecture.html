How Setu answers a customer, and when it hands them to a person

Setu is an AI customer-support desk for India. One Flask process serves three workspaces. A background worker does all the model work, so a customer never waits on it. Every model call leaves through one client that redacts personal data first, retries, stays inside a time limit, and is metered.

ScaleSeverity, per message

severity_of() · pipeline.py

Computed from the model's own readings rather than asked for as one more word, so every level comes with the rule that produced it. Higher always means worse.

Critical
  • frustration ≥ 85
  • a threat: consumer court, legal action, police, social media
  • angry and high escalation risk
goes to a person
High
  • frustration ≥ 70
  • high escalation risk, or angry
  • wants a cancellation or a complaint
goes to a person
Medium
  • frustration ≥ 35, or negative
  • frustrated or anxious
  • urgent and unhappy
the assistant answers, with a matching tone
Low
  • calm; nothing urgent
the assistant answers

Calibrated on the live model: a routine first “recharge failed, money taken” scores about 65 and stays with the assistant. The queue orders open cases by this scale, then by the longest wait.

Known limits of this build

  • It runs as one server process. The per-case locks and the worker queue live in memory, so it does not scale out across machines as it stands.
  • Street addresses are not redacted. Email, card numbers, order ids and phone numbers are.
  • The free Gemini tier allows 15 requests a minute per model, which is about three customer messages a minute.
  • orders.json stands in for a real order system.
